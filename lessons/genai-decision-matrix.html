When to pick something else

  • Agent + tools (38): when the job means doing things, such as checking an order or booking a slot, not just answering questions.
  • Prompt only (37): when you are testing an idea, the task is general, or the facts fit in the prompt. Start here before anything else.
  • Fine-tuning (30): when you need a fixed tone, format or narrow skill at scale, and you have many good example answers.
  • Train your own (21): only when you need full ownership of the model and have research-level budget, data and staff.

Notes

  • All scores are reasoned judgements for a typical company using a hosted model, not benchmark results. Your costs and results depend on your vendor, data and use case.
  • RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) searches your own documents and passes the relevant passages to the model with the question, so answers can point to a source and change as soon as the documents do.
  • Fine-tuning is better at teaching behaviour (style, format, a narrow task) than at adding new facts, and its knowledge is frozen until you train it again.
  • Control and privacy depend most on where the model runs and the vendor's data terms; self-hosting an open-weight model raises control for any option.
  • The options stack: many real systems combine a good prompt, RAG and a few tools.

Key takeaway: start with prompting, add RAG when the model needs your facts, and only fine-tune or train when the cheaper steps clearly fall short.
